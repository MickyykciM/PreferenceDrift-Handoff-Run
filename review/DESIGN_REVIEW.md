# Independent methodological review of the v17 draft, and how it was addressed

A separate reviewer agent critiqued the first draft of the v17 proposal. It had not written the draft. It worked
read-only from the handoff, the draft, and the v15/v14 source and data, and was asked to be adversarial. Its
data-based claims were then re-checked by the lead (next section). The report's sections 4–6 (`REPORT_zh.md`) are
the revised version.

## The reviewer's full text

The review follows as written; line breaks and list formatting have been adjusted for Markdown.

> **Review of the v17 fixed-prefix proposal (`draft_design_zh.md`, §4–5)**
>
> **Verdict.** The v17 structure fits the owner's §4 requirements, but I would not approve it as written. It changes
> the instrument to an "answer first" log-odds read-out, and that read-out already failed in this project (v10/v11).
> The draft also pairs one fixed sentence per condition with a control that is not a minimal pair, and uses only
> 11 families.
>
> **What I checked in the saved data** (read-only; in-memory scripts over saved JSON; nothing modified; no models run):
> - The same read-out already failed once. v10/v11 took first-token YES/NO probabilities on Qwen3-4B 4-bit with
>   label-only replies and failed development at 266/288, with opened_v7 validity at 18/24 in both orders. Two whole
>   families were confidently wrong on every item: demorgan_disjunction (6/6, −9.5 to −13.8 nats) and
>   negated_converse_application (6/6, −7.5 to −14.6). Forms like v15's were all correct. The draft does not mention
>   v10/v11 or v6.
> - The model is nearly certain on these items: median log-odds toward the correct label 19.9 nats (25th percentile
>   13.1); for 258/288 requests P(correct) was stored as exactly 1.0 (bf16 rounding).
> - A meaningless edit already moves the endpoint by about the proposed SESOI. This is a proxy only: 4-bit, a
>   different reply format, and the edit sits right before the answer. Swapping only "YES or NO" to "NO or YES" moved
>   the log-odds by a median of 1.0 nat per item; 55% of items moved at least 1 nat; the largest move was 12.9.
>   Across 38 families, the family-average shift had SD 1.8 nats and median size 1.1 nats.
> - Two draft claims check out: adding any `.py` to `experiments\` or `preference_drift\` would break v15's
>   `check_plan`, and v16's too, because v16's `sources()` calls v15's `check_plan`. The v15 timing figures also hold:
>   3.92 s mean per request, 720 input tokens, prefill about 2 s.
>
> **Prioritised problems** (the full numbered list of 22 items is summarised in the disposition table above; each
> item gave the problem, why it matters, and a concrete fix):
> 1. MUST-FIX: the exposure control is not a minimal pair for the preference sentence. Use one frame (wonder / hope /
>    believe / request), X last, equal token counts, and state that E is a leading question.
> 2. MUST-FIX: wording is a fixed effect that the interval cannot see, and C reuses the same sentences. Use 3
>    paraphrase sets, a placebo E3 vs E3′, the rule Δ − |placebo| > 0, and held-out paraphrases for C.
> 3. MUST-FIX: the SESOI does not fit the regime, so the equivalence branch is almost unreachable. Add a precision
>    table, use ≥ 20 families, require effects to exceed the placebo, and add a near-boundary secondary analysis.
> 4. MUST-FIX: the answer-first instrument is under-validated, its gate is fragile, and its fallback cannot work. Add a
>    development stage on opened prompts, use family-level eligibility, and fall back to a continuous explain-first
>    measure.
> 5. MUST-FIX: the positive-control rule contradicts the minimal plan and measures a different construct. Add a
>    P-final construct control to the core; make I-last optional.
> 6. MUST-FIX: C's novelty claim is false and D's forms are unnamed. List the items, generate C by rule, exclude
>    equivalent forms, and freeze any new operators before D.
> 7. MUST-FIX: the decision rules have gaps, a weaker confirmation bar and a discretionary branch. Use Holm on
>    one-sided t tests, add "reliable but small" and "reverse" categories, apply the same bar in C, and always run C.
> 8. MUST-FIX: the numeric specification is broken as written. Use logit differences, an fp32 final projection,
>    fp32 and determinism checks, and spaced token ids.
> 9–17. SHOULD-FIX: overlapping primaries; a format-compliance rule under treatment; π_dec is degenerate; the
>    endpoint-switch framing; factual slips; module R's acknowledgement; shared lexicons; an incomplete budget;
>    missing deliverables.
> 18–22. OPTIONAL: splits are descriptive; a v15 bridge; a preference toward the correct label; a robust secondary
>    analysis; the preference construct.
>
> **Is this a reasonable "minimal, explanatory" next step?** The skeleton fits the owner's definition. It answers
> §4 A–D and the phase-3 stop rules point by point: a shared neutral final question, an exposure-matched control,
> P1-last to separate the most recent preference from earlier ones, fixed "Noted.", family-level inference,
> discovery then confirmation, and pre-written stop rules. It also diagnoses v15 correctly: stance mixed into
> preference, escalating wording, and "reconsider your answer" said to a model that never answered. The weak point is
> the new instrument and how it is used: a deterministic read-out deep in the log-odds tail, with one sentence per
> condition, a control that is not a minimal pair, and 11 families. As written, a positive could be a wording
> artifact that C would repeat automatically, and a negative cannot reach ±1 nat. Fixing items 1–8 needs only
> protocol edits plus a development stage on already-opened data. With those fixes it becomes a sound, cheap
> (under 2 compute-hours) next step; without them I would not approve it.

Note: after the fixes, the lead's budget for the full plan is about 3 compute-hours. The increase comes from ≥ 20
families, 3 paraphrase sets, the two secondary conditions and running C unconditionally. Running only the core
conditions brings it close to the reviewer's estimate.
