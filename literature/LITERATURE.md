# Related-work verification (as of 2026-09-17)

Scope: the six references listed in handoff §7, the leads in the project's own
`docs/NOVELTY_UPDATE_2026-09-14.md`, and a targeted search around the candidate contributions of the fixed-prefix
design. Every entry below was checked against a primary page: arXiv abstract or HTML, ACL Anthology, ICLR,
NeurIPS, PMLR, AAAI or OUP pages, workshop sites, or GitHub source code. OpenReview pages and some PDFs sit behind a
browser check that was not bypassed. Claims that depend on those pages are marked **UNVERIFIED**. Prompt wording is
paraphrased, not quoted. This is a scoped check, not a systematic review, and "not found" does not prove novelty.

## A. The six references named in the handoff

**A1. Sharma et al., "Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models."**
- **Status:** ICLR 2024 main conference (poster).
- **arXiv:** 2310.13548. v1 is from 2023-10-20; the linked v4 is from 2025-05-10.
- **Authors:** arXiv lists 19 authors. The ICLR proceedings metadata lists 18: Newton Cheng is not included, and the names appear as "Tomek Korbak" and "Sam Bowman". Check the camera-ready version before fixing the author list.
- **What they did:** five assistants were tested, including llama-2-70b-chat.
  - *Answer sycophancy* is single-turn. A weakly held user belief ("I think the answer is X, but I'm really not sure", or "I don't think it's X") goes before the first answer, and first-answer accuracy is measured.
  - *"Are you sure?"* is a challenge after a generated answer.
  - *Feedback sycophancy* uses the user liking, disliking, or having written a text being evaluated. This is a preference about a reviewed artifact, not about a truth-value answer.
  - The paper also covers mimicry and an analysis of preference-model data.
- **What it lacks:** no desire/belief/instruction contrast for answers, no exposure control, and no manipulation of opinion position.
- **Relation:** this is the standard "opinion before first answer" design. A first-answer shift by itself is not a contribution.
- **Pitfalls:**
  - Cite ICLR 2024 rather than only the arXiv version.
  - "Are you sure?" is challenge-after-answer, not preference-before-answer.
- **Links:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548 · ICLR proceedings: https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0105f7972202c1d4fb817da9f21a9663-Abstract-Conference.html

**A2. Hong et al., "Measuring Sycophancy of Language Models in Multi-turn Dialogues" (SYCON Bench).**
- **Status:** Findings of EMNLP 2025, pp. 2239–2259, DOI 10.18653/v1/2025.findings-emnlp.121.
- **Authors:** the Anthology lists four (Hong, Byun, Kim, Shu). arXiv v3 onward adds Jinho D. Choi; the latest is v4 of 2026-02-26.
- **What they did:** 500 five-turn prompts in three scenarios, run on 17 models including Qwen2.5 7B, 14B and 72B.
  - In the debate scenario, the same disagreement message is repeated at turns 2–5.
  - All assistant turns are generated by the model.
  - It proposes Turn-of-Flip and Number-of-Flip.
- **Pitfall:** it is **not** the first turn-of-flip metric.
  - Li et al.'s "Average First Sway Round" (Findings of ACL 2025; arXiv v1 2025-03-28) came earlier.
  - Xu et al. (ACL 2024) already reported per-turn rates.
  - SYCON's reference list cites neither.
- **Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.121/

**A3. Liu et al., "TRUTH DECAY: Quantifying Multi-Turn Sycophancy in Language Models."**
- **Status:** arXiv only, 2503.11656 v1, submitted 2025-02-04. No venue found.
- **What they did:** TruthfulQA and MMLU-Pro multiple-choice questions. The model answers first, then gets 1, 3 or 7 templated or rationale-based follow-ups. Assistant turns are generated. Models: Claude Haiku, GPT-4o-mini and Llama 3.1 8B.
- **Pitfall:** its "answer sycophancy" is redefined as majority or outside-source claims, not Sharma's user-belief template.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11656

**A4. Montgomery, Nyhan and Torres (2018), "How Conditioning on Posttreatment Variables Can Ruin Your Experiment and What to Do about It."**
- **Status:** American Journal of Political Science 62(3): 760–775.
- **What it shows:** controlling for, dropping on, or subsetting on variables measured after treatment can bias effect estimates in any direction.
- **Relation:** in a real multi-turn design, the model's own intermediate answer is such a variable. Scripted `Noted.` turns remove that path.
- **Pitfall:** challenge-after-answer designs are not invalid; they estimate a different quantity.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12357

**A5. Dhanda, "DeltaLogic: Minimal Premise Edits Reveal Belief-Revision Failures in Logical Reasoning Models."**
- **Status:** arXiv 2604.02733 v1 (2026-04-03), single author.
  - The comments field names the ICLR 2026 Workshop on Logical Reasoning of LLMs, which is non-archival.
  - Acceptance is **UNVERIFIED**: it is not among the orals on the workshop site, and the full list is on OpenReview.
- **What it did:** FOLIO and ProofWriter items were turned into revision episodes and scored by label log-likelihood. Only 20–30 episodes were evaluated per model. Qwen3-4B scored 0.65 initially, 0.45 after revision, with inertia 0.60.
- **What it lacks:** no user opinion or pressure. The evidence changes, which is closer to the v16 branch than to the preference question.
- **Pitfall:** its bibliography miscites ReviseQA with the wrong authors, title and venue. Do not copy it.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02733

**A6. Helwe, AlRashed and Orabona, "ReviseQA: A Benchmark for Belief Revision in Multi-Turn Logical Reasoning."**
- **Status:** **confirmed** as a paper at the ICML 2025 Workshop "Assessing World Models: Methods and Metrics for Evaluating Understanding" (non-archival). It is **not** in the ICML main conference, and no arXiv version was found.
- **What it does** (from the repository code; the paper text is **UNVERIFIED**): multi-turn first-order-logic revisions, where each turn adds or removes facts or rules. The model's own outputs are appended as assistant turns, and an optional "guided" mode injects the gold label after an error.
- **Pitfall:** the repository's BibTeX is a placeholder and must not be used. It has author "Your Name", key `reviseqa2024`, a different title, and "arXiv 2024" with no ID.
- **Links:** https://openreview.net/forum?id=Z4KBiAYXlI · https://github.com/ChadiHelwe/reviseqa

## B. Closest prior work found

The letters after each entry answer four questions, where applicable:
- (a) Does it separate preference, belief and instruction wording?
- (b) Does it have a label-exposure or repetition control?
- (c) Does it manipulate the turn position of the opinion?
- (d) Does it use scripted assistant turns?

1. **Li et al., "Firm or Fickle? Evaluating LLM Consistency in Sequential Interactions"** — Findings of ACL 2025, pp. 6679–6700. https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.347/
   - Up to 8 templated challenge rounds come after the model's own answer: doubt, a belief-framed wrong suggestion, expert, consensus and emotion.
   - Experiment 1 repeats a single challenge type; experiment 2 shuffles the order of types.
   - It introduces the "first sway round", the earlier turn-of-flip metric.
2. **Xu et al., "The Earth is Flat because…"** — ACL 2024, Outstanding Paper. https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.858/
   - The Farm dataset, with up to 4 persuasion turns on initially correct questions.
   - One strategy simply re-sends the belief-framed misinformation.
   - Belief checks are kept out of the chat history, but persuasion replies are kept.
3. **Fanous et al., "SycEval"** — AIES 2025, 8(1): 893–900. https://doi.org/10.1609/aies.v8i1.36598
   - "Preemptive" rebuttals with no model answer in context produced more sycophancy than in-context rebuttals (61.75% vs 56.52%).
   - Single-turn; math and medical questions.
4. **Kim and Khashabi, "Challenging the Evaluator"** — Findings of EMNLP 2025. https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1222/
   - The same counterargument sways a model more as a user follow-up than when shown alongside the answers, and casual wording sways more than formal critique.
   - The data include LogiQA.
5. **Turpin et al., "Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think"** — NeurIPS 2023. https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/ed3fea9033a80fea1376299fa7863f4a-Abstract-Conference.html
   - A single-turn user-suggested answer, plus an "always (A)" few-shot bias.
   - Run on BIG-Bench Hard tasks, including Web of Lies and Logical Deduction.
   - This is the closest logic-domain precedent for a suggestion placed before the answer.
6. **Ben-Natan and Tsur, "Not Your Typical Sycophant"** — arXiv 2601.15436. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15436
   - v2 (2026-01-26) reports that the claim stated second wins inside a single prompt, and that recency amplifies sycophancy.
   - v3 (2026-08-30) drops the recency statement. Cite v2 for that finding.
7. **Dubois et al., "Ask don't tell"** — arXiv 2602.23971 (UK AI Security Institute). https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23971
   - A factorial design over question vs statement vs "I believe" vs "I am convinced", first-person vs "the user", and affirmed vs negated.
   - Sycophancy rises with stated certainty and with first-person framing.
   - Single-turn, subjective topics. It has no desire or instruction framing.
8. **Laban et al., "LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation"** — ICLR 2026 Outstanding Paper. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120
   - Models attempt answers too early and then over-rely on them.
   - Models over-weight first and last turns ("loss of middle turns").
   - This supports the no-self-anchor design and suggests that first and last positions may both be strong.
9. **Huang, "The yes-no bias of LLMs reflects answer order and wording, not shifts in moral judgment"** — arXiv 2607.05552. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05552
   - An arbitrary-label swap separates the verdict from a lexical pull toward "no" and a bias toward the last-printed option.
   - This is the closest precedent for a label-vs-meaning control, and it matters because target labels here are YES/NO.
10. **Sinha, "SycoBench-600"** — Findings of ACL 2026, pp. 35278–35284. https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1759/
    - Each pressure is its own branch: the question, then the model's own baseline answer, then one pressure turn.
    - Selectivity is defined as update rate minus wrong-flip rate.
    - (a) No: suggestions combine belief verbs with "reconsider". (b) No. (c) No. (d) No: the assistant turn is the model's own answer.
11. **Kumarappan and Mujoo, "Not Just RLHF"** — arXiv 2605.12991 v3. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12991
    - Pre-generated jury answers are placed in the user, assistant or tool role.
    - Wrong answers placed in assistant turns flip the model about 98% of the time, against about 76% in user turns (from the v3 HTML).
    - The project note's "early yield probes" does not match. The closest element is a logit-lens analysis.
    - (a) No. (b) No neutral repetition control: juror count mixes repetition with endorsement. (c) It varies role, not position. (d) Yes, in the assistant-role arm.
12. **Yang and Yeung, "Resist and Update"** — arXiv 2607.12985 v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12985
    - The same disagreement counts as evidence or as pressure depending only on the stated reliability of its source; the posteriors are known.
    - Mostly mechanistic, on Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Mistral-7B and Llama-3.1-8B.
    - (a) No. (b) and (c) not found. (d) **UNVERIFIED**.
13. **Sadhu, Shahad and Marino, "DeReLab"** — arXiv 2608.30413 v1. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30413
    - EMNLP 2026 acceptance appears only in the arXiv comments field: **UNVERIFIED**.
    - Defeasible multi-turn reasoning. Confirmation bias is measured relative to the model's own earlier prediction; there is no user opinion.
14. **Lampinen, Dasgupta et al., "Language models, like humans, show content effects on reasoning tasks"** — PNAS Nexus 3(7): pgae233, 2024. https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae233
    - The preprint was arXiv 2207.07051, by Dasgupta et al., under a different title. This is a citation trap.
    - Content that fits the logic improves reasoning, which supports the use of fictional records.

**Background (verified):**
- Perez et al., Findings of ACL 2023: a user biography with an opinion before subjective multiple-choice questions.
- Wei et al., arXiv 2308.03958: synthetic data reduces sycophancy.
- Laban et al., FlipFlop, arXiv 2311.08596: 46% flips after "Are you sure?".
- Zhao et al., ICML 2021 ("Calibrate Before Use"): majority-label and recency bias in few-shot prompts. PMLR lists "Zihao Zhao"; arXiv lists "Tony Z. Zhao".
- Griffin et al., arXiv 2303.06074: illusory truth in GPT-3 simulated participants.
- Schuster et al., arXiv 2601.03746: mere repetition reverses source preferences in 13 open-weight models.
- Tang et al., SPINE, arXiv 2609.09090: up to 25 adaptive turns; adaptive users beat pre-written scripts. This is an external-validity caveat for any scripted design.

## C. Candidate contributions, one by one

| Candidate | Prior coverage | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Opinion stated before the first answer shifts it | Sharma §3.3, Perez, Wei, Turpin, SycEval (preemptive), Dubois | **Covered**; not a contribution by itself |
| Preference vs belief vs instruction as separate factors | Belief strength and person (Dubois; SYCON's third-person mitigation); statement vs question (Dubois); casual vs formal (Kim and Khashabi). A "want the answer to be X" framing was not found; SycoBench mixes belief with instruction | **Apparently not addressed**. Frame the instruction arm as an instruction-following reference, not as sycophancy |
| Repetition control matched on the number and position of label mentions, without stance | Repetition as a treatment (Xu; SYCON debate; Li experiment 1; juror count in Not Just RLHF); repetition effects (Griffin, Schuster, Zhao) | **Not found** |
| Early vs final position with an identical final turn | Within-prompt order (Ben-Natan and Tsur v2); loss of middle turns (Laban 2025); challenge-type order after an answer (Li); few-shot label recency (Zhao) | **Not found** in this multi-turn, fixed-final-turn form |
| Scripted, content-free assistant turns (no self-anchor) before the first answer | Nearly all multi-turn work uses generated turns. Partial precedents: Xu keeps belief checks out of the history; SycEval's preemptive rebuttals; Not Just RLHF shows that *answer-bearing* scripted assistant turns are strong anchors | **Not found**. Keep the content-free `Noted.` in every arm, including the exposure control |
| Label vs meaning within a sycophancy design | Outside sycophancy: Turpin (letters, always-A), Zhao, Huang (arbitrary labels) | **Not found** within sycophancy |
| Formal-logic / fictional-record domain with evidence held fixed | Evidence-change revision (DeltaLogic, ReviseQA, DeReLab); content effects (Lampinen/Dasgupta); suggested answers on logic (Turpin BBH; Kim and Khashabi LogiQA) | **Not found** for user preference with fixed evidence |
